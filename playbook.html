DM-A · Force Check

Paste into the DM
Hi! Thanks for the comment. Here's the short version of my Force Check — 5 yes/no questions:

1. End of a work day: do your thumbs or wrists ache, tingle or hurt?
2. When you work deep, does the pressure come mainly from your thumbs and fingers?
3. When a client asks for more pressure, is your first move to press harder?
4. Mid-treatment, do you lose track of which foot your weight is on?
5. Asked to take an extra client or stay late, do you almost always say yes?

0–1 yes: sustainable. Keep the volume where it is.
2–3 yes: you're borrowing from your body. It's tolerating you — for now.
4–5 yes: you're burning it. Cut one client a day this month before anything else.

Fix one thing first — #3. When they want it deeper: go slower, change the angle, let your body weight sink in. Your hands just steer.

(Not medical advice — pain over two weeks, see a doctor or physio.)

DM-B · Hands & Feet Starter Map

Paste into the DM
Hi! Thanks for the comment. Two spots from my Hands & Feet map to try:

Three rules first:
- No thumb-jabbing. The pressure is your body weight shifting; the hand just makes contact.
- Slow. Hold each spot 5 breaths and wait for the tissue to let you in.
- Ask. Sensitivity varies a lot — tell them to speak up if it's too much.

HAND — the thick muscle between thumb and index finger. Client's palm down; your thumb sinks in from the back of the hand at 45° toward the bone, not straight down. Pinch, then sit back to load it. 5 breaths, release slowly. Often feels achy or tingly — say so before you start.

FOOT — the natural hollow in the front third of the sole. Client prone. Thumb over thumb, sink toward the top of the foot by leaning in, not pushing. 5 breaths, 3 times.

That's the whole method: position, angle, weight. Check your scope of practice; avoid pregnant clients and anything acute.

DM-C · Pre-Shift Check

Paste into the DM
Hi! Thanks for the comment. This is the 3-minute check I do before my first client — 11 years in, hands still fine.

1. Hands: open, fist, wrist circles ×3. Anything stiff or sore?
2. Sleep: under 6 hours = low-power mode today. Note it.
3. Food: look at today's schedule and circle 15 minutes to eat. No gap? The schedule is wrong, not you.
4. Heaviest client: who'll take the most out of you? Know which slot, save energy for them.
5. Weight: stand up — which foot is it on? Every bit of pressure today starts there, not from your thumbs.

All clear: work as usual. "Usual" doesn't mean add a client.
1–2 flags: forearm or elbow instead of thumbs for deep work, 2 extra minutes between clients.
3+ flags, or already in pain: maintain, don't push. No add-ons, no extensions, less deep work. Three red days in a row isn't a body problem — it's a scheduling problem.

DM-D · Boundary Scripts

Paste into the DM
Hi! Thanks for the comment. Here are the boundary lines I wish I'd had back when I said yes to everything.

Three rules:
1. You're declining the shift, not the person.
2. Don't give reasons, give an alternative. Reasons get argued with; alternatives don't.
3. Can't say no on the spot? "Let me check and get back to you." The gap is what stops the pressured yes.

Manager adds a client: "I'm fully booked today and the quality would drop. I have space tomorrow afternoon — can we put them there?"

Asked to stay late: "I'm good until six. Past that isn't fair to the later clients. I can stay an extra hour on Thursday."

A regular pushing for extra time: "I'll do the areas you need most properly. For the full thing, let's book 90 minutes next time — that beats squeezing it in today."

Before any yes, ask: am I actually able, or just uncomfortable saying no? If you can't tell, it's the second.

DM-E · Body Journal

Paste into the DM
Hi! Thanks for the comment. This is the 3-line journal that got me through my worst stretch — sore everywhere, hair falling out.

Every night, under 2 minutes:
Line 1 — Body: where did you feel it most today? Part + one word. "Right wrist, tight."
Line 2 — Energy: was today a +, 0 or −? One reason. "−, fifth client was deep plus extra time."
Line 3 — Boundary: did you say yes to something you didn't want to? Write it. This line matters most.

Sundays, 10 minutes, read the week:
- Which body part showed up 3+ times? That's a mechanics gap, not "normal soreness."
- What do the − days have in common? Client count, a technique, no lunch?
- How many Line 3s? Over three, your problem isn't stamina, it's boundaries.

Pain, fatigue and mood aren't the enemy. They're data — once you see the pattern, you can change it.

(Not medical advice — ongoing pain or low mood, talk to a professional.)

The red lines

These apply everywhere, every time.

  • No claims about results. Never treat / cure / heal / fix your pain. You talk about position, angle, force, and how someone works.
  • No diagnosing. If someone describes symptoms, use the line in the first toggle below.
  • No course talk and no prices — unless they ask first.
  • No patient testimonials. Don't quote clients as proof.
  • The hair loss story is only ever “it came back once I changed how I lived and worked.” Never that anything cured it.
  • Don't argue. One round, then stop.
