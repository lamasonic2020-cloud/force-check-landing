Thirty seconds, once per Reel. This is the part that quietly breaks.

You never ask for it and you never handle it. You send the link; they fill in the form on the landing page themselves; the material opens on the next screen. Their name and email land in the Force Check (Nana) Google Form under Responses, and Cody works from that.

If they DM you first

Someone who messages the account for the first time already has the link — the instant reply sends it automatically, before you've even seen the message. So don't paste it again. It reads like you didn't look at what they wrote.

Answer the actual question they asked, in one or two lines, like a colleague. Only mention the link if they ask where it went or say they can't find it.

The link changes when the new landing page goes live. When Cody sends the new URL, it needs swapping in three places: the DM above, the auto-reply in Meta Business Suite, and your bio.

3 · The landing page

They arrive from your DM, your bio, or the Story sticker. They put in their name and email, and the material opens on the next screen. You'll see them in the Force Check (Nana) Google Form under Responses, but you don't need to check it — Cody does, weekly.

The red lines

These apply everywhere, every time — comments, DMs, captions, stories.

  • No claims about results. Never treat / cure / heal / fix your pain. You talk about position, angle, force, and how someone works.
  • No diagnosing. If someone describes symptoms, use the line in the first toggle below.
  • No course talk and no prices — unless they ask first.
  • No patient testimonials. Don't quote clients as proof.
  • Your own hair loss is a story, never evidence. You may say: you lost hair in patches during your worst stretch of overwork, and it grew back after you changed how you lived and worked. That's the whole story, and it's yours to tell. You may never say, or imply, that acupuncture, massage, or anything you teach treated it, cured it, or made it grow back — and never that it will do the same for anyone else.
  • Don't argue. One round, then stop.

Nana has the same list in Chinese as a separate private note. The English one here is what goes into anything public.

Timing and volume — when to sit down and reply

You are not on call. Nothing here has to be answered quickly.

You sit down with the phone twice a day, and each sitting is about ten minutes:

  • Once before your first client of the day.
  • Once after your last client of the day.

That's it. Ten minutes is how long the sitting lasts, not how fast you have to reply. Don't pick the phone up between clients, and don't answer the moment a notification comes in.

The only standard is 24 hours. Every comment and every DM gets an answer within a day. Anything that arrives after your evening sitting is tomorrow morning's job — that is normal and it costs you nothing.

If a sitting runs long because there's a lot in there, stop at the end of it anyway and take the rest at the next one.

Two things are tied to the day a Reel goes out:

  • Be back in the comments within an hour of posting. The first hour is worth the most. You already know this one.
  • Check the Story sticker actually opens the page. Tap it yourself once, from your own phone.
